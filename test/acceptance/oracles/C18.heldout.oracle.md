# C18-H Oracle (held-out variant) — Process loss at each persistence boundary

Same guarantee under test as C18, different identities, different event wording, different ordering of the kill points, different revisions and hosts. Outcomes were fixed before any implementation observation.

## 1. Setting

Organisation `tenant-sablefin`. Two people: `u.dara.okonjo` signs off writes on the emitting side, `u.piet.vandal` operates the receiving side. Two service principals do the machine work: `svc.quill-emit@cinder` on `host-cinder-07` (computer A, store `store-cinder-main`, credential `cred-cinder-w4`), `svc.tally-intake@drift` on `host-drift-11` (computer B, store `store-drift-main`, credential `cred-drift-r2`). The two stores share nothing. Exchange runs over `chan-tidewater`.

Work lives in `repo-tidewater-api`, scope `repo-tidewater-api:lib/sync/`, base revision `r-5f02`, head revision `r-be71`. A second repository `repo-saltmarsh-console` (scope `repo-saltmarsh-console:app/reports/`, revision `r-2ad9`) exists in the organisation; `svc.tally-intake@drift` holds no grant on it. Emitting sessions are `sess-C2`, then `sess-C5` after restart; receiving sessions are `sess-D9`, then `sess-D12`.

Persistence boundaries, named here as `W1`..`W7`: `W1` the record is committed to the emitting store; `W2` the send queue entry is committed; `W3` the serialized bytes and their digest are durable; `W4` the transport reports the handoff succeeded; `W5` the receiving side commits the validated record; `W6` the receiving side updates its derived index; `W7` the emitting side files the receipt.

Digest algorithm: `sha-256` over raw bytes, declared in advance. The emitting side must also declare, in advance, whether `W1` and `W2` commit as one transaction or whether `W2` alone is the durability boundary; the expectations for `X-A6` below branch on that declaration and on nothing else.

Process loss means `SIGKILL` against the OS process followed by a cold start against the same on-disk store. Dropping a socket, cancelling a call or invoking a graceful shutdown path is a different experiment and is recorded separately as `H-CLOSE`.

## 2. Query surface the assertions read

`records(store, id)`, `current(store, scope)`, `flow(id)` on each side separately (state + attempt list + receipt reference), `trail(id)` (every attempt and transition including the ones that ended in a kill), `gate(action, id)` returning allow/deny with a reason code, and `ops()` returning queue depth and age, gaps, retries, failures, rejected records, index freshness and scope denials.

Flow states: `held_local`, `enqueued`, `serialized`, `handoff_unknown`, `handed_off`, `taken_in`, `indexed`, `receipted`, plus `conflicted`, `refused`, `abandoned_partial`. A side that cannot say `handoff_unknown` does not satisfy this case.

## 3. Ordering of the trials

The kill points are exercised from the far end backwards, so that a system which only handles the early boundaries fails early rather than late.

1. **H-1** `X-A7` — kill the receiving process after `W6`, before the receipt reaches the emitter.
2. **H-2** `X-A6` — kill the emitting process after `W1`, before `W2`.
3. **H-3** `X-A3` — kill the emitting process after `W3`, before `W4`. Payload carries a UTF-8 byte-order mark and a CRLF terminator; expected length 131 bytes, digest `sha256:2956dd5a…70a24`.
4. **H-4** `X-A2` — kill the receiving process after `W5`, before `W6`.
5. **H-5** `X-A4` — kill the emitting process in the middle of `W4`, arranged so the receiving side already holds the bytes durably and the emitter never learns it.
6. **H-6** `X-A1` — kill the emitting process after `W2`, before `W3`.
7. **H-7** `X-A5` — kill the receiving process mid-`W5`, leaving a torn record.
8. **H-8** `X-A8` — kill the emitting process after `W7`; the restart must change nothing.
9. **H-9** replay `X-A4` twice more, byte-identical, from `sess-C5` into the same live receiving process and store that served the earlier trials.
10. **H-10** submit `X-A9` under the identifier already used by `X-A3`, with different bytes.
11. **H-11** while the receiving side is still draining its recovery backlog, push `X-B1` (`repo-saltmarsh-console:app/reports/`) down `chan-tidewater`.
12. **H-12** `X-C1` runs end to end with no interference.
13. **H-13** `X-D1`, a `note` written by the emitting model summarising the morning's run, is filed after the trials above and before the final reads.
14. **H-CLOSE** repeat trial 3 with only the socket closed and the emitting process left alive.

Each trial runs to quiescence (120 s with no state change, 900 s hard cap) before its assertions are read.

## 4. Required outcome

**Preservation.** Every identifier present in `records(store-cinder-main, ·)` before a kill is present after the restart (set equality per trial). Every event whose emitting-side state is `receipted` has exactly one record in `records(store-drift-main, ·)` with a matching digest, and the pairing is 1:1 across the whole run. `X-A8` is `receipted` before its kill and still `receipted` afterwards, with the same receipt reference and no new attempt added by the restart.

**Uncertainty.** Immediately after the restart in H-5 and before reconciliation completes, `X-A4` reads `handoff_unknown` on the emitting side — not `handed_off`, not `enqueued` — and `trail("X-A4")` carries the interrupted attempt with an unknown-outcome termination. After reconciliation it is `receipted` with a single record on the receiving side, and the earlier uncertain attempt is still in the trail. `X-A5` after H-7 is either absent and re-drivable or present and complete with the emitter's digest; a readable record whose bytes differ from the emitter's while being reported as taken in is a failure. `X-A2` after H-4 is visible in `records` and `current` on the receiving side even while its index entry is missing, and the index lag or rebuild shows in `ops()`. `ops()` enumerates every incomplete effect it still carries, with a state, an age and a reason, and that count equals the count derivable from `flow(·)` across all events.

**Boundary branch.** If `W1`+`W2` were declared atomic, H-2 must be demonstrably unreachable (injection there yields both or neither) and `X-A6` ends `receipted`. If `W2` alone was declared, `X-A6` may end `held_local`, must then appear in `ops().gaps` with an explicit reason, and must not read `receipted`.

**Replay.** After H-9, `records(store-drift-main, "X-A4")` returns exactly one record; `current(store-drift-main, "repo-tidewater-api:lib/sync/")` contains it exactly once; no derived counter, relation or index entry for it has multiplicity above one; `trail("X-A4")` shows three delivery attempts of which two ended as suppressed duplicates; and each replay returned the same receipt identity as the first intake. Suppression that leaves no trace fails this trial even though the counts are right.

**Bytes.** `X-A3` as stored on the receiving side is byte-identical to the emitted source: mark present, CRLF intact, 131 bytes, digest `sha256:2956dd5a…70a24`. Re-serialization after the emitter restarts yields the same digest, or the event is marked as having unverified exported bytes rather than asserting equality it cannot support.

**Conflict.** `X-A9` ends `conflicted`; the record already taken in under that identifier is unchanged; both digests remain readable through `trail`; the rejection appears in `ops()`.

**Gates.** `gate("publish_sync_fix", "X-A4")` while that event reads `handoff_unknown` returns deny with a reason naming delivery uncertainty — not allow, and not deny-because-not-found. `gate` on `X-A6` while it reads `held_local` returns deny citing undelivered work. No gate verdict rests solely on an index entry for an event whose durable record is missing.

**Positive control.** `X-C1` reaches `receipted` on the emitting side and `indexed` on the receiving side, exactly one record, digests equal, and `gate("publish_sync_fix", "X-C1")` returns allow. If this fails, the run is void and reports nothing about crash handling. After every restart a fresh legitimate write still completes end to end.

**Negative outcomes.** At no sampled instant during trials 1–9 does any event read `receipted` on the emitting side while `records(store-drift-main, id)` is empty; this is sampled continuously, not only at quiescence. `X-B1` is never taken in, never appears in the receiving side's current view, index, cache or diagnostic output, and does appear in `ops().scope_denials` — draining a recovery backlog is not an exemption. `X-D1` changes no flow state, no receipt, no gate verdict and clears no entry from `ops().gaps`, and its evidence class remains model inference wherever it is rendered. No output anywhere claims exactly-once network delivery; idempotent application is the most that may be claimed. `H-CLOSE` is never counted as satisfying the process-loss requirement: if `H-CLOSE` passes while trial 3 fails, the verdict for trial 3 is fail, and a suite in which the two are indistinguishable has not tested restart.

## 5. Verdict

C18-H passes only when every statement in §4 holds, the durability boundary was declared before the run, the two-computer topology was real or its substitution was stated as such, and trials 1–8 used real process kills. A single instance of `receipted` without a matching received record is an immediate rejection. A single cross-scope record on the receiving side is an immediate rejection.
